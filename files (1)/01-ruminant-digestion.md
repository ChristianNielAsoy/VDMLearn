## Overview

Cattle, carabao, goats and sheep are **foregut fermenters**: microbes ferment feed in a large chamber before the true stomach. This is why they can live on fibrous forage.

## Key terms

reticulorumen, ingesta, rumination (cud chewing), eructation (belching), volatile fatty acids (VFA), esophageal groove, non-protein nitrogen (NPN), papillae, laminae, abomasum

## Content

**Four compartments** (flow order: rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum)

| Compartment | Key points                                                                                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Rumen       | Largest; fermentation vat; lining papillae enlarge the absorptive surface                                                |
| Reticulum   | Honeycomb lining; heavy foreign objects settle here (hardware disease); contractions mix ingesta and start regurgitation |
| Omasum      | Leaf-like laminae; absorbs water and some minerals/VFAs                                                                  |
| Abomasum    | The glandular ("true") stomach: secretes HCl and pepsinogen; chymosin in the young                                       |

**Fermentation**

- Anaerobic, warm, pH roughly 6 to 7, buffered by large volumes of saliva (bicarbonate, phosphate).
- Bacteria, protozoa and fungi break down cellulose and starch into VFAs (acetate, propionate, butyrate), CO2 and methane.
- VFAs are absorbed through the rumen wall and are the main energy source.
  - Acetate: fat synthesis (milk fat precursor).
  - Propionate: glucose precursor (liver gluconeogenesis).
  - Butyrate: largely converted to ketone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate) by the rumen wall.
- High-forage diets favor acetate; high-grain diets shift toward propionate.
- Gas is removed by **eructation**. If it cannot escape, the animal bloats.
- Microbes flow onward and are digested in the abomasum and small intestine: **microbial protein** is a major protein source. Microbes can use NPN such as urea, and they synthesize B vitamins and vitamin K.

**Rumination:** regurgitate a bolus, re-chew, re-salivate, re-swallow. Coarse fiber stimulates it.

**Young ruminants:** the **esophageal groove** reflex sends milk straight to the abomasum. The rumen is small at birth and develops as solid feed is eaten.

**Comparison**

- Monogastric (pig): simple stomach, enzymatic digestion; some fermentation in the large intestine.
- Hind-gut fermenters (horse, rabbit): fermentation in cecum and colon.
- Avian: crop (storage), proventriculus (glandular), gizzard (muscular grinding), ceca.

## High-yield

- Abomasum = the glandular stomach.
- Foreign body settles in the reticulum.
- Sudden grain overload gives rapid acid production and a low rumen pH (acidosis).
- Flow order: rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum.

## Mnemonic

**R-R-O-A** is the order of flow: Rumen, Reticulum, Omasum, Abomasum.
