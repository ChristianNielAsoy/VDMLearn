## Overview

Breeding programs depend on how traits are inherited and how much of the variation is genetic.

## Key terms

gene, allele, genotype, phenotype, homozygous, heterozygous, dominant, recessive, test cross, incomplete dominance, sex-linked, heritability (h2), selection differential (S), response to selection (R), heterosis

## Content

- **Segregation:** each parent passes one allele per gene. **Independent assortment:** genes on different chromosomes assort independently.
- **Aa x Aa:** genotypes 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa; phenotypes 3 dominant : 1 recessive (see diagram).
- **Test cross:** unknown dominant-phenotype animal x homozygous recessive. Any recessive offspring means the unknown is heterozygous.
- **Dihybrid (AaBb x AaBb):** 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypes.
- **Incomplete dominance:** the heterozygote is intermediate (e.g., black x splashed white Andalusian fowl give blue; blue x blue gives 1 : 2 : 1).
- **Sex linkage in chickens:** females are ZW and males ZZ. The barring gene is dominant and Z-linked. A non-barred male (ZbZb) crossed with a barred female (ZBW) gives barred sons (ZBZb) and non-barred daughters (ZbW), so chicks can be sexed by plumage.
- **Heritability:** h2 = additive genetic variance / phenotypic variance. Traits with low h2 (fertility, litter size) respond slowly; high-h2 traits respond faster.
- **Response to selection:** R = h2 x S, where S is the mean of the selected parents minus the population mean.
  Example: h2 = 0.30, S = 5 units: R = 1.5 units per generation.
- Inbreeding raises homozygosity; crossbreeding can give heterosis (hybrid vigor), strongest in low-h2 traits.

## High-yield

- Aa x Aa: 3:1 and 1:2:1.
- Dihybrid 9:3:3:1.
- R = h2 x S.
- Birds: females ZW, males ZZ.

## Mnemonic

**R = h2 x S** ("Response equals heritability times Selection differential").
