## Overview

Enzymes and cells work in a narrow pH range, so the body uses buffers and the lungs and kidneys to hold blood pH steady.

## Key terms

pH, [H+], strong acid, weak acid, buffer, pKa, bicarbonate buffer, acidosis, alkalosis, compensation

## Content

**Chemistry**

- pH = -log10 [H+]. Each pH unit is a 10-fold change in [H+].
  - [H+] = 1 x 10^-3 M gives pH 3. [H+] = 4 x 10^-8 M gives pH 7.40.
  - pH 3 is 100 times more acidic than pH 5.
- At 25 C, pH + pOH = 14.
- Buffer: a weak acid and its conjugate base resist pH change. Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]).
- Dilution: C1 V1 = C2 V2 (e.g., 25 mL of 2 M stock makes 100 mL of 0.5 M).

**Physiology (mammalian blood)**

- Bicarbonate buffer: pH = 6.1 + log([HCO3-] / (0.03 x PCO2)).
- Normal example: HCO3- 24 mmol/L, PCO2 40 mmHg: 24 / 1.2 = 20; log 20 = 1.30; pH about 7.40.

| Disturbance           | Primary change | Compensation                                    |
| --------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Respiratory acidosis  | PCO2 up        | Kidneys retain HCO3- (days)                     |
| Respiratory alkalosis | PCO2 down      | Kidneys excrete HCO3- (days)                    |
| Metabolic acidosis    | HCO3- down     | Hyperventilation lowers PCO2 (minutes to hours) |
| Metabolic alkalosis   | HCO3- up       | Hypoventilation raises PCO2                     |

- Examples: diarrhea loses HCO3- (metabolic acidosis); vomiting of stomach contents loses HCl (metabolic alkalosis); hypoventilation causes respiratory acidosis.
- Uncompensated examples: HCO3- 12 with PCO2 40 gives pH 7.10; HCO3- 24 with PCO2 60 gives pH 7.22.

## High-yield

- Compensation moves the other component in the same direction as the primary change.
- Lungs compensate fast; kidneys compensate slowly.
- pH is logarithmic.

## Mnemonic

**ROME:** Respiratory Opposite (pH and PCO2 move opposite), Metabolic Equal (pH and HCO3- move together).
